Good afternoon, we are Wed_team3. Scale AI and Appen both sell AI training data, Layer 3 of the AI stack. In 2024 the market grew 25%, yet Scale AI grew about 14.5% while Appen shrank 14.2%. Our question: why did two firms in the same growing industry perform so differently? We answer in three steps: how AI changed the industry, how each firm's resources and organization fit that change, and what each firm should do next.

AI affects this industry in two directions at once. It creates demand: LLM developers need RLHF, expert-written data, model evaluation and red-teaming, which pushed the market to $3.188 billion in 2024, up 25.2%. And it substitutes for the old work: auto-labeling, synthetic data and self-supervised learning reduce routine manual labeling. Both lead to one result: the key success factors move from labor scale and low cost to expertise, technology, data quality and organization. We use these four factors to compare the firms later.

Market growth does not make the industry easy. In FY2024, buyer power and rivalry were strong, so attractiveness is only medium. Looking ahead to 2025–2029, three AI-related dynamics matter: substitution gets stronger, specialized experts gain bargaining power, and buyers stay strong because Big Tech builds in-house. Regulation on data sovereignty, gig labor and privacy adds cost for cross-border crowd models. So profit goes to firms with technology, expertise and quality.

VRIO asks whether a resource is valuable, rare, costly to imitate, and whether the firm is organized to capture its value. Scale's RLHF and model evaluation capability meets all four: a sustained advantage. Its workforce and client relationships give at most a temporary advantage. Appen's multilingual data is valuable and partly rare, but the O column is only partial: after losing Google, Appen itself said it needed to improve delivery, crowd management, leadership and sales. Traditional crowdsourcing now gives only parity. So the decisive column is O. The next question is why.

Scale AI shows dynamic capabilities: it began with autonomous-driving data, moved into generative AI and RLHF, and by 2024 was doing model evaluation and government AI testing. Each time it sensed new demand and reconfigured. Appen shows core rigidity: its global crowd was the reason for its success, and the volume-based model built around it made the move to expert tasks hard; its platform rebuild only started in August 2024. Scale AI's task is to sustain its advantage, and each recommendation comes from a finding. R1: RLHF and evaluation is its only capability meeting all four VRIO criteria, and GenAI demand is growing. So Scale should package model evaluation and red-teaming as recurring services and organize experts into domain pools such as law, medicine, finance and coding. R2: the Congruence Model showed contributor friction, and labor regulation is a threat. Scale should review worker classification and pay, audit subcontractors, use experts only where needed and automate routine work: lower legal risk and lower cost. R3: buyer power is strong and revenue depends on a few frontier labs. Scale should grow its existing government and defense work, win enterprise clients in other industries, and invest in data security and privacy.

Appen is our most important case, because it must rebuild, and the key principle is: don't imitate Scale AI, build on Appen's own assets. R1, workforce: core rigidity and the people–task mismatch show the crowd is not set up for expert work. With limited resources, Appen should borrow rather than build or buy experts, through non-equity alliances with universities and professional associations, train existing contributors, and create a tiered workforce with fair pay and realistic deadlines. R2, positioning: Appen is stuck in the middle but owns valuable multilingual resources, so it should focus on multilingual LLM evaluation, reinvest cost savings in technology and the platform, and judge performance on quality and high-value revenue, not just cost. R3, clients: the Google loss and buyer power show the danger of concentration. Target mid-market, healthcare and finance, serve Sovereign AI projects with in-country workers, and move to recurring contracts through Quality Flow and Build My RAG. Evidence: 500+ languages and over a million contributors, 37 new customers in China, Japan and Korea in H1 2024, and non-Google revenue up 16%.

Appendix A1 · Full PESTEL Analysis

Macro Trends Favor Expert, Compliant Providers over Cross-Border Crowds

Political

  • AI techno-nationalism and data-sovereignty rules restrict offshore data and labor
  • Geopolitical labor risk in gig networks raises compliance costs
  • Government AI subsidies and public datasets (US, EU, Korea)

Economic

  • GenAI capital inflow drives expert data demand: market $3.188B, +25.2% (2024)
  • Cost pressure favors hybrid auto-label + human review
  • FX (Appen pays globally) and funding climate (Scale raised ≈$1B in 2024)

Socio-cultural

  • Concern over hallucination and bias raises the value of diverse, human-verified data
  • Fair-labor issues in the gig economy raise ESG risk

Technological

  • Shift to RLHF and subject-matter-expert labeling (coding, medicine, law)
  • Synthetic data and auto-labeling raise productivity but threaten manual labeling

Environmental

  • Data-center energy use from large multimodal datasets (limited direct impact)
  • Clients demand greener infrastructure and ESG compliance

Legal

  • Copyright suits (e.g., NYT v. OpenAI) require licensed, traceable pipelines
  • Unclear IP ownership of annotated data
  • GDPR / CCPA: PII de-identification and consent
Opportunities: RLHF and expert labeling, public AI investment, bias mitigation. Threats to crowd models: data sovereignty, labor regulation, synthetic data.
Source: Report pp. 3–4; MarketLine (2025); Sawers (2024); McKool Smith (2024).

Appendix A9 · Analysis Limitations

What This Analysis Can and Cannot Claim

Scale AI financials are estimates
Revenue and growth come from third-party sources (Sacra); trends are read cautiously.
Scale AI profitability is undisclosed
No profitability comparison between the two firms is made.
Revenue ÷ industry size is a rough proxy
An upper-bound indicator, not verified market share: firm revenue includes services outside the defined market.
Google's exit directly hit Appen
Part of Appen's 2024 decline is a client loss; AI-driven change does not explain the whole gap.
FY2024 scope
Later developments (e.g., Meta's 2025 stake in Scale AI and CEO change) are post-period and excluded from the assessment.
Frameworks are qualitative
VRIO ratings, strategic-group placement and positioning are judgments, not proven causal relationships.
